% ABSTRACT
% =============================================================================
\begin{abstract}
\vspace{-1.5mm}
Alignment in large language models is still predominantly \emph{static}: training-time methods such as RLHF and DPO imprint a single, fixed behavioral policy into the weights, making post-deployment policy updates slow, expensive, and brittle. We introduce \textbf{ECLIPTICA}, a framework that reframes alignment as \emph{instruction-driven} and \emph{runtime-controllable}: models should accept natural-language \emph{alignment instructions} that modulate behavior on-the-fly, enabling interactive alignment correction under evolving policies, user roles, and safety requirements.

We instantiate ECLIPTICA with \textbf{CITA} (\textbf{C}ontrastive \textbf{I}nstruction-\textbf{T}uned \textbf{A}lignment), an algorithm that unifies supervised fine-tuning and contrastive preference optimization with an \emph{explicit, mandatory} KL regularizer:
$L_{\textsc{CITA}} = L_{\textsc{SFT}} + \lambda_1 L_{\textsc{DPO}} + \lambda_2 L_{\textsc{KL}}$.
The KL term is not optional: it anchors the model to a reference policy to prevent mode collapse and enables stable \emph{instruction-conditioned} switching rather than shallow instruction compliance.

\textls[0]{To measure \emph{instruction-alignment} (deep policy embodiment) versus mere instruction-following, we introduce the \textbf{ECLIPTICA benchmark}, a controlled benchmark of $3{,}000$ test cases ($300$ prompts $\times$ $10$ instruction types) where only the alignment instruction varies. Experiments on \textbf{Llama-3.1-8B} across five deterministic benchmarks (ECLIPTICA, TruthfulQA, Conditional Safety, Length Control, LITMUS) show that \textbf{CITA achieves \colorbox{bg-green}{86.7\%} instruction-alignment efficiency}---outperforming DPO (56.1\%) by 30.6 pp, GRPO (36.1\%) by 50.6 pp, and PPO (20.4\%) by 66.3 pp. On TruthfulQA, CITA shows \textbf{54$\times$ stronger adaptation} than DPO (+0.054 vs +0.001). Overall, ECLIPTICA + CITA advance alignment from \emph{one-policy-per-checkpoint} toward \emph{switchable, instruction-governed} behavior suitable for modern agentic deployments. To foster further research in this domain, we make the \href{https://github.com/kapilw25/finetuning_evaluation}{code} and \href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/kapilw25/ISD-Instruction-Switch-Dataset}{dataset} publicly available.}
\end{abstract}

\begin{defin}
\scriptsize
\vspace{-2mm}
\begin{itemize}[left=-4pt,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt,parsep=0pt]

\item[\faBolt] \textbf{\textit{TL;DR}}:
\textbf{ECLIPTICA is a framework for \emph{instruction-driven, runtime-switchable alignment}};
\textbf{CITA is the algorithm that makes the switch \emph{stable}} via contrastive preference learning \emph{plus} a \textbf{mandatory} KL anchor.

\item[\faGlobeAmericas] \textbf{\textit{Why now (agentic reality)}}:
Modern deployments require \textbf{one model} to exhibit \textbf{different alignment postures} across workflows (e.g., customer support vs.\ internal research vs.\ compliance).
Static alignment hard-codes a single policy; ECLIPTICA instead treats \textbf{alignment as a controllable instruction channel} at inference time.

\item[\faCompass] \textbf{\textit{Framework (ECLIPTICA)}}:
We shift alignment from \textbf{training-only} to \textbf{dialogue / instruction-driven correction}:
a model receives \textbf{natural-language alignment instructions} that \textbf{modulate policy on-the-fly}, enabling \textbf{policy updates without exhaustive retraining}.

\item[\faCogs] \textbf{\textit{Algorithm (CITA)}}:
CITA operationalizes ECLIPTICA with a \textbf{unified objective} that couples response quality, preference contrast, and stability:

\vspace{-0.7em}
\[\boxed{
L_{\textsc{CITA}} \;=\; L_{\textsc{SFT}} \;+\; \lambda_1\,L_{\textsc{DPO}} \;+\; \lambda_2\,L_{\textsc{KL}}.
}
\]
\vspace{-1em}

Crucially, \textbf{KL is explicit \& mandatory}: it \textbf{prevents mode collapse}, \textbf{bounds preference margins}, and preserves a \textbf{switchable} policy manifold.

\item[\faRandom] \textbf{\textit{Instruction-conditioned switching (not prompt hacks)}}:
CITA is designed so the \textbf{same user prompt} can yield \textbf{meaningfully different behaviors} \emph{solely} due to the alignment instruction
(e.g., safety-first vs.\ professional vs.\ creative), enabling \textbf{multi-tenant} and \textbf{role-aware} deployment.

\item[\faVial] \textbf{\textit{New diagnostic (ECLIPTICA benchmark)}}:
We introduce the \textbf{ECLIPTICA benchmark} to isolate instruction effects:
\textbf{keep prompts constant}, vary \textbf{only} the alignment instruction.
ECLIPTICA is a \textbf{full-factorial} benchmark with
\(\lvert\text{ECLIPTICA}\rvert = 300 \times 10 = 3{,}000\) test cases, enabling direct, controlled comparisons.

\item[\faBalanceScale] \textbf{\textit{Key distinction: following vs.\ alignment}}:
We explicitly test whether models exhibit \textbf{instruction-following} (surface format compliance)
or \textbf{instruction-alignment} (internalized, robust policy embodiment consistent across contexts).

\item[\faChartLine] \textbf{\textit{Results (switchability without judges)}}:
On \textbf{Llama-3.1-8B}, across \textbf{five deterministic benchmarks},
\textbf{CITA achieves \colorbox{bg-green}{86.7\%} instruction-alignment efficiency}---outperforming DPO (56.1\%) by 30.6 pp, GRPO (36.1\%) by 50.6 pp, and PPO (20.4\%) by 66.3 pp.
On TruthfulQA, \textbf{CITA shows 54$\times$ stronger adaptation} than DPO (+0.054 vs +0.001).

\item[\faTools] \textbf{\textit{Practical upshot}}:
ECLIPTICA + CITA move from \textbf{one-policy-per-checkpoint} to \textbf{switchable, instruction-governed alignment}:
a single deployed model can be \textbf{re-scoped} by policy instructions for safety level, tone, verbosity, compliance emphasis, or creativity---\textbf{without} spawning separate aligned models.

\item[\faCode] \textbf{\textit{Release}}:
\textbf{Code:} \url{https://github.com/kapilw25/finetuning_evaluation};
\textbf{ECLIPTICA benchmark:} \url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/kapilw25/ISD-Instruction-Switch-Dataset}.


\end{itemize}
\vspace{-2mm}
\end{defin}
