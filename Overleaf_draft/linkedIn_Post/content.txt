🎯 Building an RL algorithm that outperforms DPO, PPO & GRPO—harder than we expected

Anyone who's worked in Reinforcement Learning knows the pain: reward hacking, training instability, hyperparameter sensitivity, and the constant question—"Is my improvement real or just noise?"

We spent months trying to build an alignment method that could beat established baselines. Here's what we learned:

The brutal reality of RL research:
• PPO requires 4 models in memory (policy, value, reference, reward)—our A100 40GB wasn't enough for the given batch size
• GRPO's group-relative advantages are elegant on paper but incredibly sensitive to group size (K=6 worked, K=4 didn't)
• DPO looks simple but the implicit reward model assumption breaks down when you need dynamic behavior

What finally worked (after many failures):
We kept asking: "Why can't the model just follow alignment instructions at inference time?"

Turns out, existing methods learn a fixed policy—they ignore instructions because they were never trained to condition on them.

Our solution (CITA) adds two things:
1. Instruction-conditioned preference pairs (I, X, Y+, Y-)
2. Mandatory KL anchor—not optional like vanilla DPO—keeps policies in a shared trust region

Training insights that surprised us:
• CITA achieves 25% higher reward margins than DPO (7.5 vs 6.0)—more "headroom" for policy switching
• DPO's training curves with vs without instructions nearly overlap—it literally ignores them
• We ran 13 Optuna trials to find the right KL weight, β, and learning rate—Trial 7 hit the Pareto frontier sweet spot (margin=7.52, accuracy=89%)

The honest results:
CITA: 86.7%
DPO: 56.1%
GRPO: 36.1%
PPO: 20.4%

We're not claiming CITA is universally better—PPO and GRPO still shine for online learning. But for instruction-switchable alignment, the gap surprised us.

What we still don't understand:
• Why does mandatory KL matter so much? (Our ablation shows λ=0 completely breaks switching)
• Can this scale beyond 8B parameters?
• How do we handle adversarial instructions?

Seeking your expertise:
1. What's missing in our evaluation?
2. Are there RL tricks we should have tried?
3. Where would runtime-switchable alignment actually be useful in production?
