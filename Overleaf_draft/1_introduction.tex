\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) achieve alignment through preference optimization methods like RLHF~\cite{ouyang2022training} and DPO~\cite{rafailov2023direct}. However, these approaches embed \textit{static} alignment objectives directly into model weights, fundamentally lacking flexibility for dynamic policy changes at deployment time.

\textbf{The Core Problem.} Current alignment methods optimize for a fixed behavioral policy during training. Once deployed, the model cannot adapt its alignment posture based on contextual requirements---a critical limitation for real-world applications where safety policies, regulatory constraints, and user preferences vary across contexts~\cite{kirk2024benefits,bai2022constitutional}.

\textbf{Our Solution.} We propose \textbf{CITA (Contrastive Instruction-Tuned Alignment)}, a framework enabling \textbf{instruction-driven alignment} at inference time. Unlike static alignment, CITA allows models to dynamically switch behavioral policies based on natural language instructions provided with each query.

\vspace{-2mm}
\subsection{Key Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{CITA Framework}: A unified training objective combining SFT, contrastive preference optimization, and \textit{mandatory} KL regularization for instruction-conditioned alignment. The key insight is that KL regularization---optional in DPO---becomes essential for stable behavioral switching.

    \item \textbf{Instruction Switch Dataset (ISD)}: A diagnostic benchmark of 3,000 test cases (300 prompts $\times$ 10 instruction types) designed to isolate alignment instruction effects from prompt variation. ISD enables systematic evaluation of instruction-awareness beyond surface-level instruction-following.

    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Evaluation}: Experiments on Llama-3.1-8B~\cite{dubey2024llama} across 5 benchmarks demonstrate CITA outperforms DPO in 3/5 metrics. On TruthfulQA~\cite{lin2022truthfulqa}, CITA improves by +0.054 while DPO shows minimal change (+0.001).
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{-3mm}
\subsection{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Instruction Tuning.} FLAN~\cite{wei2022finetuned}, T0~\cite{sanh2022multitask}, and subsequent work~\cite{wang2023self,iyer2022opt} demonstrated that instruction-following during training improves zero-shot generalization. However, these approaches focus on task generalization rather than alignment conditioning.

\paragraph{Preference Optimization.} RLHF~\cite{ouyang2022training,stiennon2020learning} uses reward models trained on human preferences. DPO~\cite{rafailov2023direct} eliminates the need for separate reward models but lacks instruction-awareness---both approaches embed static alignment into weights.

\paragraph{Safety Alignment.} Constitutional AI~\cite{bai2022constitutional}, PKU-SafeRLHF~\cite{ji2024pku}, and red-teaming approaches~\cite{perez2022red,ganguli2022red} focus on making models safer but assume a single, fixed safety policy. Recent work on context-dependent safety~\cite{bianchi2024safetytuned} highlights the need for adaptive policies.

\paragraph{Dynamic Alignment.} Controllable generation methods~\cite{keskar2019ctrl,dathathri2020plug} and prompting techniques~\cite{wei2022chain,kojima2022large} offer some behavioral control but operate at the surface level. CITA differs by training models to internalize instruction-conditioned alignment, achieving deeper behavioral switching.

\textbf{Positioning.} CITA bridges instruction tuning and preference optimization by training models to condition their alignment behavior on natural language instructions. This enables a new paradigm of \textbf{interactive alignment} where policy changes can be expressed without retraining.
